组织

大道行思
元智慧
发布日期:   2026-10-20
更新日期:   2026-10-29
文章字数:   3k
唯一词数:   1839
阅读时长:   13 分

入职满月，无论作为公司整体的一员，还是作为边缘观察的新人，亦或是作为体验过中美两国生活的个体，对于组织本身 我都有了一定见解。下面结合学生时代一些直白的困惑与成为打工人之后的反思写下此篇对此话题的认识。

值得一提的是，本文所讨论的组织都是自然人尺度下长期稳定的组织。层次上，我们按组织从小到大进行探讨，兼顾各层次之间的互动与差异。

细胞

当我第一次认真理解细胞这一角色，我便觉得很神奇。作为生命层面最小的单位，细胞拥有完整的自主性，能与外界进行物质交换，能通过代谢参与能量转换，甚至能进行自我复制，重新组织信息实现局部熵减。当然，除了少数堪称活体化石的单细胞生物，世界上大部分生物都进化成了多细胞生物。作为个体的细胞根据各自天生禀赋与触发条件，出现专业分化各取所长，分工协作面向多样的外部环境需要 形成各级tissues, organs, and systems，再各级scale up组成宇宙中最不可思议的奇迹——生命。

At the smallest scale, organization becomes visible in the cell. In a eukaryotic cell, the nucleus contains most of the cell’s DNA and helps regulate gene expression; mitochondria convert energy from nutrients into ATP, which powers many cellular processes; and the cell membrane regulates exchange with the outside world and helps receive signals. These roles are distinct, but none operates alone. The cell stays alive through constant coordination and feedback among its parts—not through a single component issuing commands to all the others.

The cell is a useful basic unit because it combines enough internal complexity to specialize with a boundary that keeps exchange and coordination manageable. As cells grow, their surface area does not increase as quickly as their volume, while distances for materials to travel also become greater. This can make exchange and coordination less efficient. There is no single ideal size for every cell, but bounded units allow larger living systems to organize through many specialized cells working together. In this sense, a cell is neither so simple that it lacks coordination nor so large that coordination becomes unmanageable.

“细胞”的另一个神奇之处在于，无论是在任何层面上以细胞作比喻，均可找到契合点。任何主体都如同细胞一般具有一定自主性，作为生命层面最小的单位，尽管形成细胞的任何子单元都不具备整体的全部图景，只是各自发挥自身作用，然而其共同主体细胞一旦稳定，便有了自身的目的性，那就是遵守并维持现状，毕竟，倾巢之下，安有完卵。相反，尽管细胞具有自主性，却又有与其他细胞展开分工协作的理由去形成更高层次的主体，毕竟只有把蛋糕做大后才能分得更多蛋糕。然而一旦某些细胞因为或内或外的原因，选择不再屈从于更大的主体而将自身自主性至于主体自主性之上 想以公济私，便诞生了闻之色变的——癌细胞。

虽然细胞只是生命的基本单位，我们到今天也未完全明白其所有机理，新的理论层出不穷，与组织学高度神似。因此，这个类比绝不仅局限于此，它会一直贯穿全文乃至在任何组织相关的理论中若隐若现。Anyway, let’s move upward at the moment, when many basic units work together toward shared goals, advantage coordination alone is no longer enough. The larger the organization becomes, the more it needs stable roles, procedures, and lines of responsibility.

官僚

How to mobilize cells and organize resources into a business venture is an evergreen topic. Proven by trial and error, bureaucracy is the underlying methodology behind all the solutions. 任何组织要想长久稳定运行，就必须将业务流程化，将title层级化，将人事网格化，否则，因人设岗特权特办只会快速消磨信誉，最终只会”眼看他起高楼，眼看他楼塌了”。这也就是”打江山易，守江山难”的底层原因。无一例外，千百年来，官僚一直是规模的一体两面，形影不离。

Bureaucracy does not usually appear because an organization sets out to become slow. It emerges as the organization expands. As work becomes specialized and decisions affect more people, rules, records, roles, and approval processes help coordinate activity, preserve institutional knowledge, and make outcomes more predictable. Bureaucracy is therefore not merely an organizational defect; it is part of the framework that allows an organization to scale.

然而，一旦稳固的官僚机制被建立起来，拥有自主性的基本单元——细胞便会开始琢磨并优化自己在其中的位置。遵守着不言而喻的公理，大吃小，强胜弱，高压底，组织往往形成一些看似令人费解的引理。比如任何组织倾向于扩大自身，以增强其在更大尺度官僚中的话语权，部门组长努力招人以提升自身在公司中的话语权，公司则倾向于扩张业务以争抢市场份额，国家亦持续求培育新的增长点以强化其在世界舞台上的叙事主导权。再比如，所谓的”职级通胀”指的是随着组织的长大，往往会诞生更多更复杂的title，相同的title含金量往往又在持续贬值。结合上一条引理并不难理解，从更高层面看，组织本身的扩张也是其与更大整体边界的重新划分过程，随着更多社会资源由外部转为组织内部，组织必须要持续完善其组织方式以整合这些新疆界。这在政党扩张的过程中体现最为明显，最初的成员是由纯粹的革命理想聚集在一起，但随着影响力扩张，成员的吸收必定会在熵增定律的影响下掣肘质量和数量两方面，若是一味迁就扩张速度，质量上就必然回归均值甚至导致革命理念对现实的妥协。所谓”早革命不如晚革命，晚革命不如反革命”就诠释了为维持组织快速扩张稳定性而做出物质与职级方面的牺牲。由此可见，组织太顺也不容易，而官僚制，就是顺风局最自然省力的框架。

However, the people inside that framework remain individuals, with different interests, judgments, and interpretations. Information can be delayed as it travels through layers; procedures can multiply as each layer seeks to reduce risk; and decisions can slow when responsibility is shared across many roles, which also spares the margin for corruption(贪腐). Bureaucratic delay is thus a by-product of the very structures that enable large-scale coordination, which is also the most usual and most frustrating excuse encountered in emails.

To handle bureaucratic delay, the challenge is not simply to remove the skeleton, but to keep it from becoming so rigid that it restricts the organization’s ability to act. Let’s return to the analogy of biological cells. Instead of treating bureaucratic delay as an invader from outside like virus, why not treat them as “信息脂肪”. It’s the element we must have to protect our skeleton supporting our whole giant system and cushion any deadly impacts. But we should never have too much of it, which increases the risk of malfunction or cardiovascular disease, which is the top villain in medicine—much more severe than virus or cancer. Therefore, what we can do is to maintain the dynamical balance to minimize the threats to our general health. If our health just degrades naturally as we grow bigger and older, we can manage to discover and cultivate some other younger and smaller organizations with a compared better ROI. This is also the shared incentive behind capital investment and wealth inheritance.

有本书叫《决策的本质》，作者是美国政治学者艾利森，他就提出来，观察一个组织，甚至国家的决策，其实有三个角度：第一个角度，你可以把这个国家看成是一个单一的决策者，是在有目标、有策略、有理性地做事。比如《参考消息》或西方人写新闻，经常说”白宫认为”、”克里姆林宫认为”，就是这个视角。但还有第二个视角，国家不是一个人，它是由一大堆官僚组织拼起来的。它怎么行动，不一定是为了实现什么目标，它不过是按照套路和惯性在办事。脚踩西瓜皮，滑到哪里是哪里。还有第三个角度，国家是一个竞技场。决策者不是统一的整体，而是一群独立参与者。国家怎么行动，不一定是精心算计出来的最优战略，而是掌权者内部几股势力互相博弈、讨价还价之后形成的结果。所谓”国家意志”，是对这种博弈结果的确认和解释。

If bureaucracy shows us what organizations need in order to grow—and what that growth can cost—then the idea of the goal invites a further question: what kind of society should those organizations ultimately help us build?

Utopia

The word Utopia was popularized by Thomas More’s 1516 book, which imagined an island society organized differently from the Europe of his time. Its name plays on the Greek ideas of ou-topos, “no place,” and eu-topos, “good place”: an ideal society that may be nowhere to be found, yet gives people a way to imagine what could be better. Utopia is both a dream and a critique—a vision of a more just order that also exposes the shortcomings of the world we inhabit.

尽管我们期待乌托邦，我们仍需立足现实去讨论构建它。在市场机制下，每个组织都有自己的组织方式，有相对优势也有相对成本，作为一个稳定的整体不应存在漏洞可钻。即使有，那必然会处在组织不断完善的法规禁令之中——被重新界定在组织之外。因此，不必羡慕嫉妒其他稳定的组织，任何美好的商品都必然在背面标好了价格。在充分竞争的条件下，这份价格不会容忍长期存在的利润，而行政手段的粗暴干预，在历史上只会带来整体效率与公平的丧失。Therefore, a card or badge isn’t a token of a real Utopia, but another 围城 in the same world. Then, what is a real Utopia today?

From a modern perspective, Utopia need not mean a perfect society designed in advance and imposed from above. It can instead serve as a shared standard against which we examine real institutions: Do they coordinate people effectively without erasing individual agency? Do their rules distribute responsibility fairly, and can those rules be questioned when circumstances change? A modern organization may never resolve every tension between efficiency, fairness, freedom, and stability. But the ideal remains useful precisely because it keeps those tensions visible. It turns the question from “How do we make the organization bigger?” to “Bigger for what, and better for whom?”

所谓”故乡”,不过是祖先流浪的最后一站。真正的故乡是灵魂得以安放,尊严得以守护的地方。哪里利于你,哪里就是故乡。

As far as I am concerned, an Utopia should coordinate both 细胞 and 官僚, satisfying both intentions and status quo. Since cells are qualified with full autonomy, then no judgement from others or any social consensus. Only then can the freedom of Utopia be possibly realized. As bureaucracy bridges a single cell with a greater cause, then it has to be tolerated to sustain itself in the long run. Apart from both aspects, we need to channel our organization’s mission to align with the least common values of all the people involved. Because we need both the compelling stories that unite us as we explore the cognitive frontier in pursuit of a heaven beyond, and the rational mathematics that keep us from overleveraging ourselves into a hell within.

If I were to sketch my own Utopia, it would not abolish organizations; it would make them porous rather than possessive. Every person would be empowered to work as a freelancer—to live wherever they choose, join or leave any organization by choice, and carry their skills, reputation, and basic protections with them. Rich, trustworthy market color—clear signals about demand, compensation, risks, and the people behind a project—would help each person navigate opportunity without surrendering autonomy to opaque hierarchies. The point is not to turn everyone into a lone atom, but to let cells cooperate, affiliate, and separate without being trapped, abandoned, or limited by proximity. Bureaucracy would remain as a light skeleton: enough to enforce agreements, protect common goods, and support people between projects, but never so heavy that anyone needs permission to move.

Perhaps we will never reach a frictionless Utopia: people will still disagree, markets will still misprice things, and some form will still ask us to upload a PDF of the form itself. But a wiser future can make exit possible, entry voluntary, and rules answerable to the people who live under them. The best organization is not the one that never loses a cell, but the one people choose to join, can afford to leave, and might happily return to.
